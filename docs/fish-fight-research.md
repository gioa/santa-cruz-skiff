# Fish fight evidence and calibration

Researched 2026-09-27. Primary sources below support body size, swimming capacity, tackle mechanics and a dated California regulations snapshot. None provides a calibrated, species-by-species curve of hooked line force in newtons. Simulated force, fatigue, strike probability and fight duration must be described as model choices, not measured facts. A fish's mass is not its pulling force: buoyancy supports most body weight underwater, while propulsion, water resistance, acceleration, tackle and line geometry determine line load.

## Biological anchors

| Fish | Verified observation | What the simulation can reasonably infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small rockfish | CDFW's Central California hook-and-line monitoring caught blue rockfish as short as 13 cm. The sample includes juveniles, not only trophy fish. [CDFW Nearshore Fishes](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Nearshore) | Sample ordinary small fish frequently. With adequate tackle, steady retrieval should be sufficient; do not impose a minimum stamina-depletion minigame. This ease-of-retrieval rule is a simulation choice, not a measured force result. |
| Larger vermilion rockfish | A NOAA-hosted 2022 study of the Southern California Bight's 2014 survey reports mean length 41.19 cm and mean weight about 1.35 kg. Observed female ranges were 18–56 cm and 0.12–3.58 kg; male maxima were 52 cm and 2.46 kg. The paper distinguishes true vermilion from sunset rockfish. [Keller et al., tables 4 and 6](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60776/noaa_60776_DS1.pdf) | Large rockfish can produce stronger bottom-oriented surges than small ones. Scale resistance continuously with size rather than assigning every rockfish the same boss fight. This geographically limited sample is not a Santa Cruz catch-frequency distribution. |
| Chinook salmon | NOAA gives typical mature size around 3 ft and 30 lb, with much larger exceptional fish. [NOAA Chinook profile](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/chinook-salmon) In a classic primary fishway experiment, the maximum observed Chinook speed was 21.9 ft/s (6.68 m/s), measured over a 30 ft timing zone; the authors explicitly note error from nonuniform flow. [Weaver, table 21 and discussion](https://spo.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-content/fish-bull/weaver_0.pdf) | Allow strong, short runs and recovery between efforts. The 6.68 m/s value is neither sustained cruise nor a hooked fish's spool-out speed, and should not be imposed continuously. NOAA's mature-fish average is not the mean local sport catch. |
| White seabass | CDFW's historical size-at-age table lists approximately 68.5 cm/3.0 kg, 80.8 cm/4.8 kg, 98.5 cm/8.6 kg and 106.3 cm/10.8 kg. Older age classes have small samples. [CDFW WSB FMP table 2-1](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=34151&inline=true) | These are useful size/mass anchors for substantially larger fish. Broad initial surges and slower heavy resistance are model choices; this table does not measure swimming thrust, line force or exhaustion time. |
| Pacific bonito | Primary swim-tunnel work used 45–50 cm fork-length fish at 18 ± 2 °C, tested at 0.5–1.2 m/s. Estimated optimal cruise was approximately 0.7 m/s (1.4 fork lengths/s). [Sepulveda, Dickson & Graham 2003, DOI 10.1242/jeb.00497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12847119/) Independent conference measurements report 0.5–1.4 m/s with 1.50–3.18 Hz tailbeats in 45.5–50.5 cm fish. [Dickson & Dowis, SICB primary abstract](https://sicb.org/abstracts/swimming-kinematics-of-the-eastern-pacific-bonito-sarda-chiliensis-testing-hypotheses-about-the-evolution-of-thunniform-locomotion/) | Bonito should feel like an active pelagic swimmer with repeated lateral runs and faster oscillation than a bottom-oriented rockfish of similar mass. These sustained laboratory speeds do not establish maximum escape speed or hooked force. Do not repeat the unsupported claim that bonito is warm-blooded like tuna. |

Rockfish also differ from pelagic fish in pressure response: their swim bladder expands during ascent and can cause barotrauma. A deep-caught fish should not become stronger near the surface by a universal scripted phase. [CDFW barotrauma explanation](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Groundfish/Rockfish-Barotrauma-and-Descending-Devices)

## Tackle, cues and implementation guidance

Daiwa's manufacturer manual describes drag as allowing the spool to slip under a strong pull, protecting the weakest line in the system. It separately describes free spool for lowering tackle and clutch engagement for winding. [Daiwa manual, component descriptions](https://www.daiwa.com/manual/electric-reel/british/__icsFiles/afieldfile/2017/09/01/LB500MM_British.pdf)

Shimano explains rod action through the location of blank flex and warns that a high rod-tip angle can transfer load into the fragile tip section. [Shimano manufacturer FAQ](https://fishshop.shimano.com/pages/shimano-faq)

The model should therefore compare fish resistance with current drag and retrieval capability, allow line payout only when mechanically warranted, and land a fish when it reaches the boat. Fatigue may reduce resistance but must not be a compulsory progress bar. Compute rod bend from load and rod power, spool movement from actual signed line movement, and reel sound from movement and clutch/drag state. Free-spool hiss, engaged retrieval gearing, loaded drag ratchet, and clutch clicks should be distinguishable. A stopped spool must not keep making a running-drag sound. These are design requirements inferred from mechanisms, not laboratory audio measurements.

## 2026 Santa Cruz regulation snapshot

This is a dated game ruleset, not a live regulation service. Emergency changes and quotas can supersede published seasons. Unsupported evidence remains unknown rather than becoming a violation; retention is still the player's decision.

- **Vermilion/sunset:** combined two per person in the Central Management Area; within ten total RCG fish. Rockfish have no minimum size; boat anglers must possess a descending device. Existing groundfish season and tackle checks apply. [CDFW groundfish summary](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Groundfish-Summary)
- **Chinook:** Monterey/Pigeon Point south schedule in the August 19 update is April 11–August 31, then September 1–30; closure may occur earlier under harvest guidelines. Minimum total length is 24 in through May 15, then 20 in; two daily and aboard the vessel. Coho retention is prohibited. North of Point Conception, at most one rod and two single-point, single-shank barbless hooks while fishing for salmon or fishing with salmon aboard. Non-trolled bait requires barbless circle hooks; paired hooks must be hard-tied and at most 5 in apart. A sinker over 4 lb requires the specified independent automatic release arrangement. Salmon cannot be filleted aboard. [CDFW August 19 salmon flyer](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=242581&inline=)
- **White seabass:** current Central page says year-round, three fish, minimum 28 in total or 20 in alternate length. The March 15–June 15 one-fish restriction applies south of Point Conception, not Santa Cruz. Do not implement a proposed 35 in limit as current law. [CDFW Central regulations, updated September 11](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean/Regulations/Fishing-Map/Central)
- **Pacific bonito:** ten fish, with up to five below the usual 24 in fork-length or 5 lb threshold. Do not substitute total length for fork length. [CDFW 2026 booklet, July 17 revision, §28.32](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=239985) and [CDFW current species-regulation index](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Fishing/Ocean). Retained small fish use the five-fish allowance; a single small bonito is not automatically illegal. The PDF body was fetched and read directly because the search index still labels this URL as its January edition.
- **MPA classification:** salmon and Pacific bonito are listed pelagic finfish; white seabass is not automatically included in that legal category. [Official CCR §632 definition](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Document/IF7D76610E68D11EEA00AACD3D3AE5397?contextData=%28sc.Default%29&transitionType=Default)

Only record booleans when the equipped tackle and actual capture method establish them. A generic unknown hook must not silently become barbed, barbless or circle-shaped.

## Implemented simulation choices

`pixel-fish-fight.js` scales species-specific thrust with body mass, differentiates run/rest timing, lateral movement, diving and headshake frequency, and reduces effort using work against loaded tackle. Small rockfish do not inherit the old common surge curve. Larger rockfish progressively gain stronger, longer short surges above 1.5 kg; this is a continuous calibration, not a species unlock or a forced fight timer. Landing checks boat-side geometry, current pull and line payout; it never requires a particular energy percentage.

The species sampling ranges, size-frequency exponent, mass interpolation and rarity multipliers are gameplay approximations. The new pelagic species remain uncommon; bait, layer, habitat and water temperature modify positive encounter weights. An unsuitable rig lowers probability rather than forbidding a species. The virtual starter tackle is specified as 20 lb mono with a 15 lb leader; 30 lb upgrades and a 75% effective knot assumption determine the weakest-link break load. Knot efficiency is a model assumption, not a universal physical constant or a manufacturer's guarantee.

Reproduce the engineering comparison with `node qa/pixel-fish-feedback/calibrate.mjs`. Its JSON explicitly labels simulated thrust. At the same 12 m starting depth, starter tackle and 1.2 handle turns/s, ordinary rockfish retrieve in about 15 s without slipping drag; a 5.5 kg rockfish takes about 32 s and pays about 5 m of line. Salmon, WSB and bonito produce distinct longer runs. These results arise from the model and remaining line length; they are not measured catch durations.
