# Shore encounter ecology: evidence and calibration

Reviewed 2026-09-28. Applies to Pacifica and Half Moon Bay. The five represented species are barred surfperch, striped bass, California halibut, white croaker and jacksmelt. The model uses the same separation as Santa Cruz: species habitat, feeding and presentation change **absolute encounter rates**, then a fish is selected from those same rates. It does not normalize unsuitable tackle into an equally frequent different fish.

The sources support qualitative relationships. They do not contain a measured per-second bite dataset for these two beaches. Local survey records establish occurrence, not present-day density; southern-California survey abundances are not used as quantitative Santa Cruz calibration. This is an evidence-informed game model, not a calibrated ecological population survey or a real fishing forecast. Seasonal affinity describes availability, not legal fishing seasons.

Jacksmelt retains the English common name and the display name 加州似银汉鱼, consistent with the [Academia Sinica fish-name database](https://fishdb.sinica.edu.tw/chi/chinesequer2.php?R1=&T1=&cn=&dere=asc&fm=&gc=&me=&orderby=science&page=31&pz=100&vn=) entry for *Atherinopsis californiensis*.

## Explicitly authored quantities

Every base encounter rate, monthly multiplier, bait and rig score, distance/depth envelope, feeding-layer width, day/night factor, wave response and presentation penalty in `dist/shore-fish-ecology.js` is game tuning. The five base rates are scale parameters before affinities, not per-species observed catch rates. White croaker's `0.0022` and jacksmelt's `0.0026` per-second base rates are authored values; white croaker have year-round model availability, while jacksmelt receive greater availability in April–August and remain possible in other months. The exact hook-size differences are authored approximations to the Carolina #1, fish-finder 2/0 and float-rig #6 loadouts.

The `float_rig` suspends a small bait portion at approximately 1 m below the surface where water depth permits. That fixed leader is a game equipment configuration, not a measured optimum for all jacksmelt schools, beaches or sea states. The feeding-layer factor reads the bait's actual `presentation.depth`; it does not infer bait depth from offshore cast distance. Jacksmelt do not pass through a universal bottom-contact gate. Their broad, nonzero incidental feeding-layer response allows bottom-rig catches while a suitable suspended presentation receives a greater affinity. White croaker remain bottom-oriented in this simplified model even though occasional midwater feeding is documented.

Distances are measured in metres offshore from the local waterline and evaluated against that cross-section's trough/bar position. A smooth response follows the near trough for perch, the bar/channel vicinity for bass, farther sand for halibut, sandy-bottom areas for white croaker and nearshore water for jacksmelt. These are preferred search areas, not hard biological range limits. Depth is evaluated independently and tide changes the depth available over the same bottom geometry. Longer casts can skip the suitable feeding area entirely.

The inventory contains sand crabs, squid strips and **cut** anchovy. It does not silently upgrade dead cut bait into live bait. Perch favor sand crab and the smaller bottom hook. Bass and halibut favor fish bait and the larger bottom rig. White croaker favor squid or cut fish near the bottom, with crab still possible. Jacksmelt favor a small suspended bait but can take sand crab on a Carolina rig. Incidental mismatches generally have much lower absolute rates. Unsupported bait/rig/habitat, unusable bait and dry water yield zero instead of a minimum species floor. Lack of bottom contact suppresses the bottom-oriented species; submerged jacksmelt presentations are evaluated by their feeding layer instead.

Moderate whitewater can help expose forage, while excessive orbital motion, turbulence and turbidity reduce effective feeding. Halibut receive a stronger visibility/turbulence penalty, not an absolute rough-water ban. Independently, the terminal-tackle simulation converts wave/current forcing and the rig's sinker into sinking, bottom contact, stability and drift. Both physical loss of presentation and reduced feeding opportunity affect the hazard. This deliberate separation makes waves matter to the bait as well as the fish. See [the wave model and its NOAA/NWS sources](shore-wave-model.md) for wave propagation and forcing; fish-response coefficients remain authored tuning.

## API and stochastic use

```js
shoreEncounterRates({
  sample, bait, rig, baitCondition: 1, month: 9, hour: 6,
  presentation: {depth: sample.depth, bottomContact: 1, stability: 1},
});
// {perSpecies:[{id,ratePerSecond,factors}], totalRatePerSecond,
//  dominantSpeciesId, medianWaitSeconds, status}
```

`sample` accepts shore distance, depth, geometry strengths and wave/current fields from `sampleShore`. Both short rig IDs and inventory rig IDs work. The factor breakdown is for debugging and calibration; the ordinary player hint describes observable water/tackle conditions without displaying exact rates. `medianWaitSeconds` is only the stationary-environment exponential median; a drifting, degrading cast has changing rates and no fixed countdown.

**How these preferences are used now.** The simulation no longer draws a hazard budget from these rates. The same preference functions are split into habitat suitability (`shoreSuitability`), feeding (`shoreFeeding`), bait appeal (`shoreBaitAppeal`) and scent/visibility. These inputs drive the agent-based schools described in [the population model](fish-population-model.md), so bites emerge from where schools are and how the bait is presented. `shoreEncounterRates` remains as a preference index for tests and debugging; nothing in play samples it.

## Reproducible validation

Run `node --test tests/shore-fish-ecology.test.js tests/shore-smallfish.test.js` for independent checks of bait, hook, distance, depth, season, waves and presentation preferences. Run `node --test tests/shore-technique.test.js tests/fish-population.test.js` for the population behaviour: soaking beats recasting, the right hook and trough beat an oversized hook or maximum range, and float versus bar-gap anchovy decide the species.

Run `node scripts/calibrate-shore-population.mjs 40 --json reports/shore-population-calibration.json` to replay ten fixed strategies over 40 seeds in each scene through the real `PacificaSimulation`, including cast flight, sinking, drift and bait washout. The report gives the share of runs with a bite within 2 and 10 minutes, the median wait and the species. These are game-behaviour comparisons, not measured catch rates. Current results are summarised in [the population model](fish-population-model.md).

The new small-fish catch path also verifies ordinary strike, retrieval, catch decisions and reload/sale without injecting a fish. White croaker and jacksmelt under 0.4 kg can be brought in as soon as they reach the angler; the heavy-fish stamina threshold is not required. Their represented weights, fight strengths and game credit values are authored; no observed local size distribution or market price is claimed.
