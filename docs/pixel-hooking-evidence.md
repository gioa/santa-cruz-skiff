# Hooking and fish-loss evidence

Researched 2026-09-27. This supplements `fish-fight-research.md`. Hooking, losing an already hooked fish, and post-release mortality are different outcomes. No source found supplies a universal Santa Cruz species-by-species escape percentage. Numerical bite windows, hook-seat strength, and failure hazards in the game are calibration parameters, not measured biological constants.

## Evidence usable in the simulator

| Mechanism/species | Primary or first-party evidence | Defensible application and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circle hooks | Florida FWC advises maintaining tension without a sharp hookset; after the fish takes the bait, reel it in. [FWC fishing basics](https://myfwc.com/fishing/basics/), [fish handling and gear](https://myfwc.com/fishing/saltwater/outreach/fish-handling/). Alaska DFG's staff report explains gentle continuous pressure and warns a jerk can pull the circle hook out. [ADF&G report, printed p. 181](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/regulations/regprocess/fisheriesboard/pdfs/2016-2017/uci/rc2.pdf) | Circle-hook rigs can seat through steady retrieval or fish pulling against the rod. They should not require a timed strike button. These sources do not establish a species-specific seating probability. A physically slack/free-spooled rig must not automatically seat simply because time passed. |
| Lingcod | WDFW's fishing guide recommends slowly and steadily reeling rather than a sharp set. It explains some lingcod hold the bait without being hooked and can let go near the surface; have a net ready. [WDFW, 2022](https://wdfw.medium.com/key-in-on-structure-around-puget-sound-for-hard-fighting-lingcod-b7d6074c28a2) | A reel-to-connect path is realistic for bait fishing. A poorly seated or merely bait-holding fish may be less secure than a well-hooked fish. Do not assume every lingcod requires an exaggerated hookset or every lingcod automatically loses hold at the surface. |
| White seabass | CDFW's management plan identifies a soft, easily torn mouth. [WSB FMP, printed p. 3-28 / PDF p. 58](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=34195). Its angler brochure recommends circle/barbless hooks and a soft knotless landing net. [CDFW brochure](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=36629) | Excessive load and abrupt load spikes can weaken hook purchase more for WSB than a robust generic mouth. The FMP gives no force threshold or escape percentage. Its discussion of release injury is not a landing-rate measurement. |
| Chinook salmon and hook barbs | A WDFW three-year Cowlitz River study observed 411 Chinook hooked and 345 landed. Its table reports landing fractions 0.871 with barbs and 0.782 without barbs. [Official report, Table 2, printed p. 20 / PDF p. 22](https://app.leg.wa.gov/ReportsToTheLegislature/Home/GetPDF?fileName=Cowlitz+Hooking+Mortality+Study-Proviso+Report_7e54aa82-5f97-4b75-a25e-8ca6166374cc.pdf). The subsequent peer-reviewed paper also reports evidence of reduced landing rates with barbless hooks. [Fisheries Research, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165783623002412) | Actual landing success depends on tackle, not only fish species. These observations are from a freshwater fishery with different methods and conditions, so neither fraction should be copied as a Santa Cruz ocean loss probability. This is distinct from the same paper's post-release survival estimates. |
| Salmon trolling and slack | ODFW's ocean coho guide says the boat and fish generally set the hook while trolling, recommends continuous tension, and describes fish charging the boat, jumping, or throwing hooks. [ODFW ocean salmon project leader's guide](https://myodfw.com/articles/10-tips-targeting-ocean-coho) | Trolling can seat a hook without an extra button. Rapid changes in direction can create slack. The detailed jumping behavior is documented for coho, not proof that every Chinook should leap or have identical loss susceptibility. |
| Blue, copper, and vermilion rockfish | CDFW's Monterey/Carmel hook-and-line sampling uses shrimp flies, baited shrimp flies, and bar jigs, and includes small fish. [CDFW Nearshore Fishes](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Nearshore). ODFW documents common bottomfish bait/lure methods and the need to monitor rock abrasion. [ODFW marine shore fishing](https://myodfw.com/articles/oregon-marine-shore-fishing) | Small ordinary fish on adequate tackle can be retrieved directly, without a forced fatigue or rapid-response hookset minigame. Low default loss under steady retrieval is a gameplay calibration consistent with low loads, not a published comparative landing statistic. There is no basis here for making every rockfish impossible to lose or for ranking the three species by a precise escape percentage. |
| Pacific bonito | CDFW describes strong fighting ability and capture on live bait, cast lures, and trolled metal/feather jigs. [CDFW Pacific bonito fishery account](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=34315). Existing swim-tunnel evidence is documented in `fish-fight-research.md`. | More energetic runs can create greater dynamic load/slack risk than the same-size rockfish. No retrieved evidence measures a universal bonito hook-loss probability or proves an especially fragile mouth. |
| California halibut and Pacific mackerel | CDFW documents California halibut's ambush feeding and conical teeth. Its San Francisco Bay hook study examines survival after capture and handling. [CDFW nearshore program](https://wildlife.ca.gov/Conservation/Marine/Nearshore), [halibut study](https://nrm.dfg.ca.gov/FileHandler.ashx?DocumentID=33774). No suitable primary species-specific mackerel hook-loss measurement was found. | Use conservative calibrated hook-seat/slack responses. Do not turn halibut post-release mortality into an escape chance, substitute Pacific halibut results for California halibut, or invent a measured small-mackerel escape rate. Small-fish ease should follow size, tackle and actual load. |

## Modeling recommendations

- Separate a bite from a seated hook. Reeling into a fish, steady rod pressure, or actual trolling resistance may seat it; a sharp strike is optional and depends on hook/rig type.
- For small ordinary rockfish and mackerel, make stable retrieval the normal successful interaction. No mandatory hookset reaction window and no arbitrary common random-loss timer.
- After seating, base loss on accumulated slack, hook purchase, force spikes and species/mass, instead of rolling the same escape chance for every species on every frame. Correctly set drag and consistent retrieval should visibly improve outcomes.
- WSB can be especially vulnerable to overload; lingcod can have an initially incomplete bite; active pelagic fish can generate more difficult changing loads. Values, thresholds and comparative weights remain explicit simulation choices.
- Preserve real break-load mechanics separately from hook pullout. A small fish normally cannot snap appropriately rated tackle merely because a generic tension value crosses a UI threshold.
- Do not advertise guaranteed catches, species-specific measured odds, or transferable experimental rates. A good result is species-differentiated, physically interpretable behavior with documented calibration.

## Implemented v17 model

The supplied bottom, dropper, sliding-sinker, and float rigs now explicitly use circle hooks; jig and feather rigs use J-style hooks. This specifies the game's supplied equipment, not a claim that every real rig of those names has that hook. Catch records preserve the known circle-hook property; they do not invent a barbed or barbless property.

`pixel-sim.js` chooses the fish when it bites. Both steady retrieval and the optional tightening/lifting action must produce sustained pressure with an engaged spool and sufficiently little slack before the hook seats. The optional action starts a short physical retrieval movement; it does not guarantee an instant hookup. Waiting alone, free spool, or pressing the action through a slack line cannot bypass the requirement. The former universal nine-second missed-strike failure is replaced by a separate species-dependent bait-abandonment model, with less abandonment exposure while pressure is building.

`pixel-hooking.js` draws one loss threshold for each seated fish and accumulates exposure only under sustained slack or overload, with brief grace periods. Normal steady pressure contributes no baseline random-loss risk. Hook strength and hazard weights vary with species, fish mass, and known circle-hook geometry; smaller rockfish and mackerel are forgiving, while WSB is more sensitive to overload. Head movement can amplify exposure when the line is slack or overloaded. Line breakage remains a separate weakest-line load check. Every numerical seating duration, grace period, force threshold, and hazard rate is simulation calibration, not a measured field percentage.
