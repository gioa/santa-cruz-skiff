# Independent sinker inventory and load

Phone verification: 390×844 production rod workbench on an isolated local QA origin/save. Swapped the starter 3 oz sinker to 8 oz: 8 oz stock decreased and 3 oz returned. Reload preserved 8 oz (the fixture then bought another spare). Replaced bottom rig with a new feather 4/0 rig: it arrived unweighted, while the old rig kept its 8 oz sinker. Selected 4 oz: inventory fell from 1 to 0 and the slot showed 4 oz. Screenshots record both equipped states. No browser console errors; viewport reset and QA tab closed. Fixture is not shipped in dist.

Model: bottom/slider/dropper/sabiki/4/0 feather accept separate 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 oz weights. Premade jig head and float assembly retain fixed weights. Unweighted hook/leader/bait uses a 6 g nominal effective mass, allowing slow drift rather than an arbitrary prohibition. Weight is stored on the actual rig, including when stowed, so rod switching cannot duplicate sinkers. Worn leaders return recoverable sinkers; physical break/loss and capsize remove them. Old saves preserve the weights already included with their existing rigs; no recurring spare grant. New games have a finite starter selection; new standalone rigs bought at the shop have no free sinker.

Physics: existing sink rate, horizontal inertial response, bottom friction and rod load take installed weight. Added finite winding torque and vertical water resistance during retrieval, while loose line can be wound freely. Heavier sinkers now resist retrieval and add force during fish fights. Paid line remains conserved. Force, drag and torque constants are gameplay approximations, not measured reel specifications. Uniform-depth current model is unchanged.

Evidence: ODFW bottomfish guide specifies varying sinker weights with current and distinguishes a leadhead jig from a sinker/hook rig: https://myodfw.com/articles/oregon-marine-shore-fishing (accessed 2026-09-28). RBFF explains fixed versus sliding weights and selecting weight for depth/presentation: https://www.takemefishing.org/blog/may-2021/a-guide-to-weights-and-sinkers-for-bass-fishing/ . These establish qualitative design; the game's inventory sizes, prices and rates are not source recommendations for Santa Cruz.

Tests cover stock conservation, missing/invalid stock, mounted versus stored rods, save/migration without replenishment, fixed-weight rig rejection, terminal loss and worn-leader recovery, capsize, repeat shop purchases, carried mass, sinking/current/bend/retrieval differences and frame-rate/line conservation.
